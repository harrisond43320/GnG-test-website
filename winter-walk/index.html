The Winter Walk

Riley walks your yard for about 10 minutes. Then I text you pictures of anything that could hurt it over the winter. You don't have to be home.

Reply WALK See a sample report

How it works You do nothing.

  1. Text me WALKTap the button and hit send. That's all you have to do.
  2. Riley walks your yardRiley is our general manager. The walk is on a day a crew is already in your town, and it takes about 10 minutes. You don't have to be home.
  3. I text you the picturesYou get a picture of each problem and a price for our fall package, Cut Back, Kick Back. It comes the same day or the next.

If nothing will hurt your yard this winter, I'll tell you that too. No pitch.

What Riley looks for Before winter.

Riley takes a picture of every problem on the walk.

  • Deep leaves on the grassRiley measures the deepest spots with a tape measure. Ohio State says leaves over 2 inches should be picked up or chopped up. If not, they rot right on the grass.
  • Hostas after the frostPenn State says the dead leaves can hold slug eggs over the winter.
  • Peonies with black spotsIowa State says that fungus lives through the winter in old leaves left in the garden.
  • Old iris leavesThe University of Minnesota says iris borers spend the winter as eggs on them.
  • Plants with mildewLike bee balm or garden phlox. Penn State says to cut them and throw them out. Don't compost them.
  • Bushes that got bigRiley takes a picture. If you want them trimmed, we can do it on the same visit.

Where this comes from: Ohio State Penn State Iowa State University of Minnesota
